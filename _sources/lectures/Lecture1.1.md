## Probability and statistics

> **Figure:** Two ovals. The left one is labeled "Data generating process" and the right one "observed data". An arrow labeled "Probability" runs from the left oval to the right oval. An arrow labeled "Statistics" runs back from the right oval to the left oval.

**Probability**
: Given a data generating process, what are we likely to observe?

**Statistics**
: Given observed outcomes, what can we infer about the process that generated the data?

This inference is the basis of Science!

## Common vocabulary for probability

- **Random experiment:** a mechanism that produces an outcome that cannot be predicted with certainty.
- **Random variable:** a numerical quantity that is generated by a random experiment.
- **Independence:** events $A, B$ are independent if
  $$P(A \cap B) = P(A)\,P(B)$$
- A **discrete** random variable has a finite number of possible values.
- A random variable is **continuous** if its possible values include a whole interval of numbers.

> **Figure:** Two number lines. The left one, "Possible values of a discrete R.V.", has a handful of isolated dots marked on the line. The right one, "Possible values of a continuous R.V.", has a shaded interval highlighted on the line.

### Probability mass functions

Discrete RVs have **probability mass functions (PMF)**, $P(x)$.

> **Figure:** A stem plot of a PMF. The x-axis ("Value") runs from 2 to 12 and the y-axis is "Prob." with the top tick labeled $4/36$. The stems rise from the low values to a peak in the middle (around 6 to 8) and fall off again toward 12. The shape matches the sum of two dice.

### Probability density functions

Continuous RVs have **probability density functions (PDF)**, $f(x)$.

> **Figure:** A curve with two humps (a smaller peak on the left, a larger one on the right) over an x-axis, with y-axis "Prob. density". The region under the curve between points $a$ and $b$ is shaded. The annotation reads $P(a < X < b)$ = area of shaded region.

### Mean

The **mean** ($\mu$) of a random variable $X$ is:

$$\mu = E(X) = \sum_x x\,P(x) \quad \text{(discrete R.V.)}$$

$$\mu = E(X) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,f(x)\,dx \quad \text{(continuous R.V.)}$$

### Variance

The **variance** ($\sigma^2$) of a R.V. is:

$$\sigma^2 = \sum_x (x-\mu)^2 P(x) \quad \text{(discrete)}$$

$$\sigma^2 = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} (x-\mu)^2 f(x)\,dx \quad \text{(continuous)}$$

The **standard deviation** ($\sigma$) is the square root of the variance.

### Conditional probability

The conditional probability of "A given B", $P(A \mid B)$, is the probability of observing $A$ given that we already know the outcome of another R.V., $B$.

Example:

- A: It's raining, with $P(A) \approx 0.2$
- B: It's cloudy, with $P(B) \approx 0.4$

$$P(A \mid B) \approx 0.5 \qquad P(B \mid A) \approx 0.99$$

## The normal distribution

Also called the "Gaussian distribution" or the "bell curve".

> **Figure:** A bell curve labeled $f(x)$ over an x-axis. Dotted vertical lines mark the center and points to either side, with tick labels $\mu-2\sigma$, $\mu-\sigma$, $\mu$, $\mu+\sigma$, $\mu+2\sigma$.

$$X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$$

read as "$X$ is normally distributed".

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)^2\right)$$

The "**standard normal**" distribution has $\mu = 0$, $\sigma^2 = \sigma = 1$:

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2}\right)$$

## PDFs and CDFs

- **PDF:** probability density function
- **CDF:** cumulative distribution function

$$F(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} f(x)\,dx$$

> **Figure:** Two stacked plots sharing the same x-axis. The top plot shows a bell-shaped curve $f(x)$, labeled "PDF", with the area to the left of a chosen x-value shaded and labeled $A$. The bottom plot shows the CDF $F(x)$, an S-shaped curve rising from 0 to 1 (y-axis marked 0 and 1). A dotted vertical line connects the chosen x-value on both plots, and the point on the CDF at that x is labeled $A$, showing that the CDF value equals the shaded area.

## Common vocabulary for statistics

- A **population** is a complete set of items or events of interest (that exist or could exist).
- A **sample** is a randomly chosen subset of the population.
- A **measurement** is a number or attribute associated with each member of a sample.

The set of measurements associated with a sample is called the "sample data".
